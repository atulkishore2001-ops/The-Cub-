## 4. Things to confirm

- **"droop" and "drift down":** the speech-recognition models hear "drop" for the face line, and "drop / dip / drip / drift" for the arm line. The captions use the standard FAST wording, "droop" and "drift down", which match the brief. Tell me if you hear something different.
- **"29 October":** one model hears "20th of October"; two hear "29 October", which is World Stroke Day, so the captions and card say 29 October.
- **Pronoun in the brief:** the brief refers to the speaker as "her". The notes and graphics don't refer to the speaker at all, so nothing on screen depends on it.
- **Name card:** the brief didn't ask for one, so there isn't one. If you'd like the speaker's name and designation on screen (as in the earlier reels), send them and I'll add it.
- **Length:** 78.2s rather than 75s, to keep the speaker's natural pace (see section 1). A 75s version is possible by speeding up to about 1.07×, but it would sound rushed.

## 5. Review of the raw footage

### Works well
- Clear structure (stat → definition → causes → FAST → what to do → call to action) and a calm, credible delivery.
- Clean, gated audio and a tidy background; the blue panel and plant give colour without clutter.
- A steady camera and consistent position across the three clips, so the joins are invisible.

### Could be better next time
1. **Framing is tight with the head high in the frame.** That leaves little room for graphics above the head, so cards sit over the shirt. A slightly wider shot with more headroom gives more layout options.
2. **Clip 1 is a little darker and about 3 dB quieter** than clips 2 and 3 (both fixed in the edit).
3. **Hands move into the lower frame often.** It's natural, but it competes with lower graphics; resting hands on the desk during key lines helps.
4. **A few words are hard to make out** ("droop", "drift"); a slower, deliberate read on the FAST letters helps captions and comprehension.
