## 2. Review of the raw footage: what could be better

### Works well
- **Strong, relatable opening.** Four parent worries in her first 15 seconds, each easy to picture.
- **Calm, credible delivery.** The desk, the DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 books and the plant all signal "professional", and the clean cream wall left room for text without covering her.
- **Clear structure:** concerns → what it is → signs → reassurance → what to do.
- **Steady camera, good light, clean audio.** No hum or room noise; the audio is noise-gated, so the silences were easy to cut cleanly.

### Could be better
1. **The framing is wide, so her face is small** (about 10% of the frame width). I reframed to about 1.2–1.3× and tracked her face so it sits at a consistent height. Next time, framing from mid-chest up gives a sharper image.
2. **Voice level is low** (−31 to −34 LUFS against Instagram's roughly −14) and varies by about 6 dB between sentences. Fixed by levelling each sentence; a higher mic gain would leave more headroom.
3. **The silence is fully gated** (dead digital silence between phrases), which can sound unnatural once cut. The quiet music bed covers this. Recording 5 seconds of room tone helps.
4. **Two small wording points** (section 5).
5. **"Get an assessment" has no next step.** There's no handle, phone, website or "link in bio" in the video. Put the booking route in the post caption or a pinned comment, or send it and I'll add it to the call-to-action card.

## 3. What was done

### Cut (89.5s of raw material → 69.7s of speech)
- Removed every pause and the lead-in and lead-out silence of each clip. Pauses were tightened to a short natural breath (about 0.1–0.25s). No words were removed.
- All cuts land on silence and are cross-dissolved over 4 frames.
- 1.04× playback with pitch preserved.

### Framing
- Gentle reframe (about 1.15–1.3×) with her face tracked per frame, so it stays at the same height and centred even when she leans or gestures.
- A slow push-in (about 2.5%) runs through each sentence, and the framing changes by about 5% between sentences. Both are subtle.
- All graphics sit in the clear wall area above her head, and captions sit on her torso, well below the chin.

## 5. Things to confirm

- **"aren't bright":** all three speech-recognition models hear "aren't **right**". From the meaning, she almost certainly means "bright", so the captions and the graphic say "bright". Tell me if you hear it differently.
- **Wording that differs from the brief.** The brief asks for "not a reflection of intelligence", "seek an appropriate assessment", "early support can make a meaningful difference" and "children aren't lazy; they learn differently". She says "nothing to do with intelligence", "get an assessment", "with early help, your child can do wonders" and "your child is not lazy, he just learns differently". The brief also asks that text match the dialogue, so **the on-screen text uses her words**, highlighted in the same spots. If you'd rather use the brief's phrasing for the takeaway cards, it's a quick change.
- **"forgets in exam":** kept as spoken in both the captions and the card (not "in the exam").
- **Contact route:** none was supplied (see section 2, point 5).
