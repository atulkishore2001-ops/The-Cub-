# Reference Edit Breakdown — Engagement Highlight (RVGRAPHER style)

Source: reference video (3:07, 1280×720, 25 fps, AAC stereo).
Target: recreate this style on our footage, using **the first 1:47 of `music/song_full.mp3`** (file time 0:00–1:47, saved as `music/song_first_1m47s.mp3`). This file is the reference video's own audio track (verified identical, zero offset), so file time = reference video time.

> **How to use this document:** it is a *style guide*, not a shot list. Our footage differs from the reference.
> Apply the principles: pacing per song section, transitions, where slow motion goes, and how to pick the strongest moments.
> Section 5's content column shows what the reference used; we fill each slot with whatever in our footage plays that role best.

### Selection principles (picking from raw footage)
- Take only the **best 1–3 s of each clip**: the peak of a movement (the smile landing, the twirl at full spread, a hand reaching the face), never the setup or the end of it.
- Prefer **clean, steady and in-focus moments**. Skip shaky starts, focus hunting and people looking at the camera awkwardly.
- **Slow-motion clips** go on the emotional moments and on the drop. Real-speed clips go to documentary and ritual moments.
- Each moment gets **detail → medium → wide** variety; never cut between two near-identical framings.
- **Hold longer (2 bars) on emotion and cut faster (2 beats) on energy.** Let quiet parts of the song breathe.
- Movement inside the frame should flow across a cut, and hard cuts land where an action peaks on the beat.

## 2. Shot-length rules (what the editor did)

Shot lengths are almost always a **multiple of 2 beats**:

| Section | Typical shot length | Transition |
|---|---|---|
| Intro (S 0–8) | 2.5–5 s (1–2 bars) | slow cross-dissolves, fade from black |
| Verse (S 9–41) | 2.4 s (1 bar) or 4.8 s (2 bars) | cross-dissolve (~0.4–0.6 s) landing on the bar |
| Breakdown (S 41–50) | 2.4 s per shot | soft dissolves |
| Riser (S 50–52.6) | **2–3 frames per shot** (0.08–0.12 s) | hard flash cuts — "photo burst" |
| Chorus (S 52.6–90) | 2.4 / 4.8 s | dissolves + **white flash** on accents |
| Peak (S 92–103) | **1.2 s (2 beats)** | **hard cuts on the beat** |

Cuts land on the downbeat or on beat 3 of the bar. Cross-dissolves are centred on the beat (so the detector sees them ~0.2 s early or late).

## 3. Transition vocabulary (in order of how often they appear)

1. **Cross-dissolve, 0.4–0.6 s** is the default. Used about 70% of the time, including between two shots of the same subject.
2. **Dip to black.** About 0.3 s down, 2–3 frames of black, then a ~0.5 s fade up. Used once, right before the first musical hit (V 10.9–11.5).
3. **White flash.** About 2–4 frames ramping to pure white, then back. Used on big accents: V 12.5 (first hit), V 60.7 (start of the twirl at the drop), V 75.0 (end of the entry dance).
4. **Light-leak / colour-burn.** A red-orange film-burn wash sweeps across over ~0.6 s, between two portrait shots (V 24.6–25.3).
5. **Bokeh flare overlay.** Big white out-of-focus circles wipe over the frame (V 27.0, V 30.0).
6. **Photo-burst montage.** A preview of moments coming later (ceremony, gifts, guests). Each still or clip runs 2–3 frames, inset at about 80% scale on an off-white background, with a slight jittery tilt and ghosted edges. It plays on the riser just before the drop (V 53.6–56.0, about 2.4 s = 1 bar, about 20 images).
7. **Black blink + zoom-blur punch.** One black frame, then a radial zoom-blur into a close-up (V 144.2–144.8). This falls outside our 1:47 window but works well for a ring moment.
8. **Fog / haze white-out.** The frame dissolves through soft white haze (V 138.5–139.5). Also outside our window.

## 4. Slow motion & camera motion

These come from visual inspection; the edit doesn't use speed ramps.
- Nearly all **portrait and couple shots play in slow motion**: hair, dupatta and hand movements are smooth and floaty. This looks like 50/60 fps footage conformed to 25 fps (40–50% speed).
- The **twirls, the lehenga swirl, walking feet and the dupatta throw** at the couple entry (V 56–75) are the strongest slow-motion moments. They are always placed on the drop or chorus.
- **Documentary moments** (guests arriving, gift trays carried in, hugs, rituals) are at or near real speed.
- Camera: slow gimbal/slider push-ins and lateral slides on decor and gift details, rack-focus foreground decor, low-angle tracking of feet. No whip-pans or speed ramps.
- **Detail → medium → wide** sequencing per subject (e.g., hand on necklace → seated full → close portrait).

### Speed policy for our edit (decided per clip after reviewing the footage)
Nearly everything gets some slow-motion feel; only the degree varies. Speeds below are relative to real time.

| Tier | Speed | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| Hero slow | 25–40% | Twirls, lehenga or dupatta swirls, walking feet, hair flips, petals or confetti in the air, the ring slipping on |
| Dreamy | 40–60% | Bride and groom portraits, couple romance (forehead touch, hands, glances), slow decor push-ins |
| Gentle | 60–80% | Candid laughs, couple interactions with dialogue-like motion, guest reactions, gift trays carried in |
| Near-real | 80–100% | Rituals with fast hand action (tilak, garland, sweets), hugs, the 2-beat peak montage, group photos |

Rules:
- The speed a clip can go depends on its **frame rate**. 50p can go to 50% cleanly, 60p to 42%, 100/120p to 20–25%. A clip shot at 25/30p stays at 100%, or 80% at the slowest (with frame blending), so it never stutters.
- Pace builds with the song: the intro and breakdown run in the slowest tiers, the chorus mixes hero-slow moments with gentle ones, and the peak runs near-real so the 1.2 s cuts carry energy.
- Each clip runs at one constant speed. The reference has no speed ramps, so we won't add any.
- Speech and lip movement (talking, laughing loudly) look odd below about 60%, so those clips stay in the gentle tier or faster.

## 6. Look / grade

- Warm, soft-contrast cinematic grade. Blacks are slightly lifted, highlights are rolled off, skin tones are natural and warm.
- The portrait session leans into **red/orange practical gel light** with deep shadows (moody). Ceremony footage is bright and clean but still warm.
- Shallow depth of field almost everywhere (fast primes), with foreground decor blur.
- 16:9 frame with no letterbox bars. A small studio watermark sits bottom-right through the whole film.
- No on-screen text apart from the opening credit (the names come from the real welcome board).

## 7. Ending (reference, beyond our window)

The reference ends with the group photo and then returns to the romantic red-light couple portrait for about 4 s, with a slow fade to black. Our cut-off at file 1:47 lands just as the peak section ends, so a **short ~1.5 s music fade (file 1:45.5 → 1:47)** and end on a hero couple shot with a fade to black.
